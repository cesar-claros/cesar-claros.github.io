Routine scalp EEG is the most accessible neurological recording, but deciding from a single session whether a patient has epilepsy is hard when no seizure is captured. In collaboration with [Nemours Children's Health](https://www.nemours.org/), the goal of this project is to find EEG biomarkers that characterize epilepsy and that a clinician can inspect. We use the [Temple University Hospital EEG Epilepsy Corpus](https://isip.piconepress.com/projects/nedc/html/tuh_eeg/) as development data.

**Benchmarks.** The first phase established strong classification baselines to measure the interpretable approach against. Recordings are cut into fixed-length windows, balanced and split at the subject level so no patient appears on both sides of a split. Each window is transformed with [HYDRA](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-023-00939-3), a random convolutional kernel transform, and the sparse features feed a cross-validated linear classifier whose window scores are aggregated into one prediction per subject. We compared signal representations along the way (raw referential channels versus the transverse bipolar montage, ICA-cleaned signals and brain sources selected with ICLabel and dipole fitting, and a bag-of-components representation that pools per-source features), and benchmarked pretrained EEG foundation models from the [BioFoundation](https://github.com/pulp-bio/BioFoundation) family (FEMBA, LUNA, LuMamba). We also audited dataset confounds such as sampling rate and line noise before trusting any accuracy number. These models are benchmarks for classification accuracy only; they do not explain what in the signal separates the groups.

**Interpretable approach.** The next phase adapts and implements a waveform-based pipeline built on two pieces of prior work from our group: [QSMP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15492) (Mendoza-Cardenas, Silva, and Brockmeier, MLSP 2026), which finds representative subsequences in long time series by combining Quick Shift mode seeking with the Matrix Profile, and the bag-of-waves classifiers that produced [interpretable EEG biomarkers in mouse models of neurological disease](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/ae4d8c) (Cano Achuri et al., Journal of Neural Engineering, 2026). The idea is to discover a dictionary of representative waveforms across recordings, describe each recording by how often those waveforms occur, and train a sparse classifier whose features are the waveforms themselves. We expect this approach to be less accurate than HYDRA or the foundation models. We accept that, because the biomarkers it produces can be inspected, localized, and related to clinical EEG practice.

This project is in progress. Code: [tuh-eeg-epilepsy](https://github.com/cesar-claros/tuh-eeg-epilepsy).
